What's in the box

Everything you need to draw, time, and ship a scene.

Early, but honest — here's what works today.

Draw

Seven brushes with distinct feel — Pen, G-Pen, Pencil, Marker, Airbrush, plus hard & soft erasers. Pressure-sensitive. Size 1–300 px, opacity, and a stabilizer that actually helps.

Colour

Full colour picker, eyedropper (hold Alt), and 13 curated palettes — Classic, Anime, Pastel, Vivid pop, Neon cyber, Sunset, Sakura, Retro 8-bit and more. Your custom colours persist.

Animate

Layers, onion skin (red before / green after, 0–4 frames), timeline with 1–60 FPS playback, and buttons for add, hold, delete, new drawing, and copy-held.

X-sheet

The part I'm proudest of. A real exposure sheet: numbers mark starts, lines are holds, ticks are ends. One-click retiming on 1s, 2s, or 3s.

Share

Export MP4 (H.264, plays everywhere) via FFmpeg, or PNG sequence at full resolution. Frame-range picker on both. Save and open .checkmate projects.

Polish

Animated splash screen, cached rendering so heavy frames stay smooth, and undo / redo across everything — including layer and timeline edits.

The X-sheet

Anime isn't drawn on every frame.

A drawing is held for two or three frames — animating on 2s — and studios plan that on an exposure sheet. Rows are frames. Columns are layers.

Checkmate has one, built in.

  • N A number marks where a drawing starts
  • │ A line is how long it's held
  • ✓ A tick is where it ends
  • ↕ Click to jump, drag to scrub
  • 2s One-click retiming on 1s / 2s / 3s
  • ± Hold + / Hold − to stretch or shrink a drawing

Get started

Your first animation in five minutes.

  1. 1Draw a ball on the left. Just a circle.
  2. 2Press Insert. Onion skin shows the last ball in red.
  3. 3Draw the ball a little further right, guided by the ghost.
  4. 4Repeat a few times.
  5. 5Press Space. Drag the FPS slider until it feels right. 12 is classic.
  6. 6Open the X-sheet, click 2s. You're animating on twos.
  7. 7Export MP4. You've got a video.

How it's built

Small core. Sharp tools. No magic.

Checkmate/
├─ src/
│  ├─ Checkmate.Core/       the brain: model, editing, undo/redo, save/load
│  └─ Checkmate.Desktop/    the face: Avalonia UI, MVVM, Skia, export
└─ tests/
   └─ Checkmate.Core.Tests/ xUnit tests for the core

  • Vector strokes, not pixels.

    Every line is points + pressure + brush, rendered with SkiaSharp. Clean line art, cheap undo, full-resolution exports.

  • The exposure model.

    A layer stores drawings keyed by start frame; a drawing holds until the next. Holds are free. The X-sheet is just a window onto that data.

  • A tested core.

    All editing flows through one Editor class with no UI dependencies, covered by 18 unit tests.

  • Fast while you draw.

    Finished drawings cached as bitmaps; only the live stroke renders on top. Translucent brushes and erasers still come out right.

  • Video without temp files.

    Frames stream straight into FFmpeg's input. Low FPS is written at 24 fps with repeats, so every player accepts it.

Honest limitations

What's not in the box — yet.

I'd rather you hear it from me.

  • No lasso / move / transform yet. Text can't be edited after placing.
  • No paint bucket yet — colouring is by brush. Gap-closing bucket is next.
  • No audio, camera moves, or GIF export yet.
  • Project files are plain JSON — great for diffing, big for long animations.
  • One scene per project.
  • Developed and tested mainly on Windows. Mac/Linux reports welcome.
